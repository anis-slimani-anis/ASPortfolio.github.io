SNCF Connect,
journey search redesign

SNCF Connect already writes good copy about engineering works and replacement coaches. It just never shows it where people choose their train. I redesigned the results page so travellers see the disruption, its type and the real journey time before they book.

Where it started

A usual route that stopped being suggested, and nobody said why.

The trigger came while using Citymapper: my usual route stopped being suggested and I didn't know why. It turned out the line had engineering works. The question that stuck: what if the traveller could see and shape their own trip instead of being quietly rerouted around a problem nobody told them about?

I took that question to SNCF Connect's disruption handling, where the same gap shows up at the moment people book.

Read the detail

During my studies, a seven-week team project on navigating an unfamiliar station taught me that journey planning hides smaller, harder problems underneath the obvious one.

The problem

Real information, wrong moment.

SNCF writes disruption content well, but it lives after the decision point. Travellers book first and discover the coach later.

The content exists and it is well written. It just arrives one lane down and several screens too late.

Cost for the traveller

Booking blindly, planning the wrong arrival time, and not being able to weigh price against risk.

Cost for SNCF

Calls, complaints, refunds, lost trust and lost conversion.

Read the detail

I audited three flows live on desktop, mobile web and the app: long-distance search, the results list, and the Info trafic page. Seven findings, one pattern: the content exists and is well written, but never arrives at the moment of choice.

Every observed action became a possible feature. Eleven were plotted on impact against effort. Eight were low effort, because they reuse one card, one strip and one detail sheet.

Framing

If we show the disruption directly in the results list, travellers can make an informed choice before buying their ticket, and SNCF gets fewer customer service calls.

The secondary measure moves first and can be read straight off the results list. The north star confirms later, and the guardrail says when to stop.

The redesign

One severity language at every decision moment: icon, label and colour together, never colour alone.

01

Before the results

The destination station is flagged in autocomplete, and date chips mark the days with works.

02

The result card tells the truth

A tinted strip (« Travaux, car de substitution ») and the honest duration (« 4 h 42 au lieu de 3 h 52 »), with the direct alternative right below.

03

A filter, with a way back

A switch hides disrupted journeys, and a row brings them all back. The traveller decides; the product doesn't decide for them.

Accessibility

Severity is never colour alone.

Icon, label, colour

Each severity pairs a distinct icon and a written label with its colour.

Semantic HTML

Each card is an article, the filter a real checkbox, and the detail drawer a focus-trapped dialog.

VoiceOver

Tested with VoiceOver on iPhone. Each card reads departure, arrival, disruption, duration, then price, so the disruption is never the last thing heard.

Contrast

Checked programmatically across every route, both themes and both breakpoints, against WCAG 2.2 AA thresholds.

The limits

Five tech-savvy participants. The live site was tested on Paris to Dijon and the redesign on Massy to Marseille. I moderated my own design, and some prompts were leading.

What comes next

From information at the right moment to information before the app is even open.

  1. Proactive communication: move « Suivre ce trajet » onto the card and test push notifications
  2. Show the coach stop and the walking time to it
  3. Prove it with the guardrail in a live A/B trial
